# Student assumptions

These are assumptions to validate, not claims that research has proved them.

- A student preparing for a dated exam thinks in topics, and can give a rough
  estimate in hours for each one.
- A weekly pattern of available hours is enough for a first plan; a student
  adjusts individual weeks by moving sessions rather than by describing
  exceptions up front.
- Progress feels trustworthy only when it counts study that was logged, not
  sessions that were planned.
- A student wants the planner to do the arithmetic of fitting topics into the
  time left, but will not trust a schedule that changes without asking, so
  every plan is reviewed before it takes effect.
- After missing a session, a student who already uses an AI agent is more
  likely to recover with a ready revised plan from it to accept than by
  rearranging sessions themselves.
- A plan that says plainly how much study does not fit is more useful than one
  that quietly overfills the week.

Future research may change the Product Model; this file does not override it.
