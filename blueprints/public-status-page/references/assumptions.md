# Status page assumptions

These are assumptions to validate, not claims that research has proved them.

- Visitors arrive at a status page because something seems wrong, and want the
  answer to "is it them or me?" before anything else.
- A short update posted early is worth more to a visitor than a complete one
  posted late, so operators value help writing more than help deciding.
- Operators will accept text drafted by a language model only when they can
  edit it freely and nothing is published until they post it.
- Subscribers expect to confirm an email subscription before they receive
  anything, and to leave it from any message in one step.
- Setting component status by hand is acceptable for teams that do not yet run
  automated monitoring.
- Maintenance announced ahead of time reduces the number of people who believe
  an outage is under way when it starts.

Future research may change the Product Model; this file does not override it.
